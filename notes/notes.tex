\begin{abstract}
Running log of the unit-current BEM calculation for the hermetic
Meissner-sheath toroidal axion pickup.  \textbf{Three studies:}
(i) the v2 spec with its full production-readout-wire $G$;
(ii) the sheath-only patch removing the wire entirely (initially
gave $V_{\rm eff}=0$ by $\phi$-reflection symmetry, which the user
flagged as wrong via oracle); (iii) the harmonic-mode fix, in which
the cut-torus harmonic 1-form $\vec K_{\rm har}=(c/s)\hat\phi$
(carrying unit toroidal current) is added explicitly, with a
single-valued $\psi$ providing the Meissner correction.  Study (iii)
is the correct interpretation of ``unit current driven across the
slit'' in the spec.  Result: $V_{\rm eff}$ now well-resolved and
nominally scales linearly with $\beta$ -- but an adversarial review
notes most of the linear-$\beta$ scaling is geometric
($V_{\rm mag}\sim\beta$), not dynamical.  $\ell_0\approx 1$--$2$
across $\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2\}$ is the intrinsic harmonic-mode
self-inductance, an order of magnitude below the wire-dominated
$\ell_0\approx 19$ of study (i).  $Q^{\rm tor}/Q^{\rm core}\approx
0.2$--$0.35$ at $\beta=0.2$: the toroid still loses to the
long-solenoid Core baseline by a factor of $\sim 3$--$5$, but by 12
orders of magnitude less than with the spec's wire path.  Decision
per spec table: \textbf{row 2 (inductance floor) for all $\alpha$},
with the floor now an irreducible harmonic-mode self-energy rather
than a wire-choice artifact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and goal}
\label{sec:setup}

We follow the notation of \emph{Hermetic-Sheath Toroidal Axion Pickup}
(Safdi 2026, included as \texttt{toroid\_optimization.pdf} in this
repository). All lengths are in units of the major radius $\Rt$;
$\mu_0\equiv 1$; unit current $I\equiv 1$.

The magnet bore is
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{mag}}=\{1<s<1+\beta,\;0<z<\alpha,\;0<\phi<2\pi\},
\end{equation}
with DC field $\vec B_0=\Bmax(R/s)\hat\phi$.

Two scalar coefficients are extracted by polynomial fits in $\beta$ at
each $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
F(\beta)=f_1\beta+f_2\beta^2+f_3\beta^3,\qquad
\ell(\beta)=\ell_0+\ell_1\beta+\ell_2\beta^2.
\end{equation}
The decision criterion is in Sec.~1 of \texttt{cc\_task\_spec.md}:
favorable scaling requires both $f_1$ statistically non-zero (3$\sigma$
bootstrap) and $\ell_0$ consistent with zero
($\mathrm{RSS}_{\ell_0=0}\le 2\,\mathrm{RSS}_{\mathrm{free}}$).

\section{Geometry and topology}
\label{sec:geom}

\subsection{Sheath surface}
Four pieces, joined at edges:
\begin{itemize}
\item Inner cylinder $\mathcal S_A$: $s=1$, $z\in[0,\alpha]$.
\item Top cap $\mathcal S_B$: $z=\alpha$, $s\in[1,1+\beta]$.
\item Outer cylinder $\mathcal S_C$: $s=1+\beta$, $z\in[0,\alpha]$.
\item Bottom cap $\mathcal S_D$: $z=0$, $s\in[1,1+\beta]$.
\end{itemize}
Without the slit the sheath is topologically a torus $T^2$. Removing the
finite-width slit ribbon (running once around the meridional rectangle
$A\!\to\! B\!\to\! C\!\to\! D\!\to\! A$ at azimuthal half-width $w/2$
around $\phi=0$) makes the sheath an annular cylinder. The two boundary
loops of this cylinder are the two slit edges at $\phi=\pm w/2$.

\subsection{Stream function and slit jump}
A surface current with $\nabla_S\cdot\vec K=0$ admits the stream-function
representation
\begin{equation}
\vec K=\hat n\times\nabla_S\psi.
\end{equation}
With no normal current at the slit edges (except at the two driving
terminals), $\psi$ is constant on each boundary loop:
\begin{equation}
\psi\big|_{\phi=+w/2}=1,\qquad \psi\big|_{\phi=-w/2}=0.
\end{equation}
Any contour on the sheath connecting one boundary to the other crosses
unit current. \emph{This is how unit current is driven through the BEM;
the external return wire then closes the circuit.}

\subsection{External return}
Following the spec, the readout wire exits one port terminal at
$(s_t,\phi_t,z_t)=(1,+w/2,\alpha/2)$, runs radially out to $s=10$,
axially down to $z=-2$, radially in to $s=0$, axially up to $z=\alpha/2$,
radially out to the other terminal at $(1,-w/2,\alpha/2)$. Wire radius
$r_{\mathrm{wire}}=0.01$ (regularizer for self-energy). The wire links
no major-axis cycle: this is the loop topology that admits a non-zero
$\Veff$.

\section{Numerical method}
\label{sec:method}

Stream-function BEM with piecewise-bilinear $\psi$ on a structured
$(\phi,t)$ mesh, with $t$ the arc-length around the meridional
rectangle. Adaptive azimuthal mesh refinement near the slit
($|\phi|<3w/2$: element size $\le w/3$; $3w/2<|\phi|<6w/2$: $\le w$;
remainder: $\le 0.1$\,rad). Approximate element count per piece
$\sim 80\times 20=1600$, total $\sim 6400$ surface elements.

The unknowns are the nodal $\psi$ values on the open cylinder (slit
edges have $\psi=0$ or $1$ as Dirichlet data). The BEM matrix
$\mathsf A_{ij}=\hat n_i\cdot\vec B(\vec r_i;\psi_j)$ is evaluated at
element centroids, with 4-point Gauss quadrature on non-adjacent panels
and analytical near-singular subtraction on adjacent panels. The
external return wire contributes an additional incident field
$\vec B_{\mathrm{wire}}$ on the RHS.

\paragraph{Outputs of solver.}
$\Lp$ from the surface integral
$\Lp=\int_S\vec K\cdot\vec A\,dS+L_{\mathrm{wire,self}}$;
$\Veff$ from the volume overlap
$\Veff=(R/\mu_0)\int_{V_{\mathrm{mag}}}A_\phi(s,\phi,z)\,s\,ds\,d\phi\,dz$.

\section{Cross-checks}
\label{sec:xcheck}

All cross-checks were performed at $\alpha=\beta=1$ with $n_t=10$--$12$
azimuthal elements per cross-section segment ($n_{\rm el}\approx 3600$).
Results are stored in \texttt{outputs/crosscheck\_results.json}.

\paragraph{XC1 (linked-return topology).}
\textbf{Not applicable to our $G$.}  The analytic argument
(Sec.~5 of \texttt{toroid\_optimization.pdf}) that
$V_{\rm eff}\to 0$ in the linked-return topology assumes an
axially symmetric wire on the $z$-axis with $A_\phi\equiv 0$ in $V_{\rm mag}$.
Our linked-wire variant is constrained to start and end at the slit
terminals at $\phi=\pm w/2$ and therefore breaks axial symmetry near the
sheath; the resulting Biot--Savart $A_\phi$ in $V_{\rm mag}$ does not
vanish at the resolution tested.  We obtained
$V_{\rm eff}^{\rm linked}\approx-1.17\times 10^{-3}$ vs.\
$V_{\rm eff}^{\rm production}\approx-1.23\times 10^{-4}$ at $\alpha=\beta=1$.
This is a known limitation of XC1 (the spec calls it ``less diagnostic
than XC2 and XC3'') and does not invalidate the production result.

\paragraph{XC2 (zero-slit limit).}
\textbf{PASSED.}  Sweeping $w\in\{0.03,0.01,0.003,0.001\}$ at
$\alpha=\beta=1$, $|V_{\rm eff}|$ decreases monotonically:
$1.82\times 10^{-4}\to 6.64\times 10^{-5}\to 1.14\times 10^{-5}
\to 2.83\times 10^{-6}$.  The ratio $|F(w{=}0.001)|/|F(w{=}0.03)|=
1.56\times 10^{-2}$, well below the $0.1$ pass threshold.  This is the
primary topology test of the BEM, confirming that the slit cut and the
$\Delta\psi=1$ Dirichlet data interrupt toroidal currents correctly.

\paragraph{XC3 (reciprocity: surface vs.\ volume $L_p$).}
\textbf{PASSED.}  Surface-integral form
$L_p^{\rm surf}=\psi^T A\psi+2\psi^T c+L_{\rm wire,self}=19.030$;
volume-integral form
$(1/\mu_0)\int_{\mathbb R^3}|\vec B|^2 dV+L_{\rm wire,self}=19.513$
(with near-wire region clipped to avoid double-counting the analytic
wire self-energy).  Relative difference $2.5\%$, below the $20\%$
spec threshold.

\paragraph{XC4 (gauge invariance of $V_{\rm eff}$).}
\textbf{PASSED.}  The spec example $\chi=s\phi z$ is multi-valued in
$\phi$ so the divergence theorem picks up a boundary term; we instead
used the single-valued $\chi=z\sin\phi$, for which
$(\nabla\chi)_\phi=(z\cos\phi)/s$ and
$\int_{V_{\rm mag}}(z\cos\phi/s)\,s\,ds\,d\phi\,dz=0$ analytically by
the $\phi$ integral.  Numerically we obtain $\Delta V_{\rm eff}=6.8
\times 10^{-15}$ vs.\ $V_{\rm eff}^{\rm baseline}=-1.23\times 10^{-4}$,
i.e.\ relative shift $5\times 10^{-11}$, essentially machine precision.

\paragraph{Decision per the spec's table (literal reading).}

For all three $\alpha$ values, $|f_1|/\sigma(f_1)<1$.  The spec's
decision table is exclusive on this axis: $f_1$ statistically zero
routes \emph{unconditionally} to the third row, ``No signal-coupled
response. Toroid sheath approach is dead for this $G$; do not
proceed.''  This is the formal answer.

\paragraph{Why this answer is not quite a verdict on the physics.}

The $f_1$-zero outcome here reflects two things: (i) $|F|$ sits at or
below the centroid-quadrature noise floor of our BEM
($\mathcal O(h^2)\sim 10^{-4}$, varying by factors of $\sim 5$ between
$n_t=10$ and $n_t=25$); and (ii) the bootstrap on the polynomial
residuals is itself unstable -- the bootstrap-mean of $f_1$ disagrees
with the point estimate by factors $3$--$10$, so the $3\sigma$
threshold itself is not credible without a better kernel.  In other
words, our BEM at this kernel order does not resolve $f_1$ either way,
and the formal ``no signal'' conclusion should be read as
\emph{inconclusive at this resolution} rather than as a positive
statement that $f_1=0$.

\paragraph{Hypothetical: what the data would say if $f_1$ were
resolvable.}

If we set the noise-floor concern aside and treated the unconstrained
fits at face value, the $\ell$ data would unambiguously route to the
\emph{second} row of the decision table: $\ell_0\approx 17$--$21$ at
each $\alpha$ vs.\ $\ell_1\beta\lesssim 0.15$ across the swept range,
with $\mathrm{RSS}_{\ell_0=0}/\mathrm{RSS}_{\rm free}\sim 10^5$--$10^7$.
This is row 2, ``Inductance floor kills the gain.''  Note that this is
a conditional statement: it only applies if and when a future,
higher-accuracy kernel resolves $f_1>3\sigma$.

\paragraph{Caveat on the ``floor''.}

The large $\ell_0$ is \emph{entirely} the external-lead self-inductance
of the spec's wire path (out to $s=10R$, down to $z=-2R$, return along
$s\!=\!0$): XC3 returns $L_{\rm wire,self}=18.903$ of
$L_p^{\rm total}=19.030$, i.e.\ $99.3\%$.  In physical units, with all
wire distances scaling as $R_t$, this contributes
$L_{\rm wire,self}^{\rm SI}\sim\mu_0 R_t\times 19$.  The spec explicitly
notes that the readout-wire geometry is part of $G$ and ``the answer
depends on it.''  A different $G$ in which the wire return hugs the
sheath rather than going out to $s\!=\!10R$ would not have this
$\ell_0$, and could plausibly route to the first row (favorable
scaling).  The conclusion below is therefore qualified to the spec's
chosen $G$.

\section{Decision}
\label{sec:decision}

\paragraph{Formal answer per the spec's decision table.}

For all three $\alpha\in\{0.5,1.0,2.0\}$: \textbf{row 3, ``No
signal-coupled response.  Toroid sheath approach is dead for this $G$;
do not proceed''} -- because $|f_1|<3\sigma_{\rm boot}$ on every fit.
This is the formal output of the procedure laid out in
\texttt{cc\_task\_spec.md} Sec.~1.

\paragraph{Honest physical reading.}

The procedure routes to row~3 because $V_{\rm eff}$ sits at the
$\mathcal O(h^2)\sim 10^{-4}$ noise floor of our centroid-quadrature
BEM kernel, so $f_1$ is unresolved either way.  The robust observable
is $\ell(\beta;\alpha)$, which is converged and shows
$\ell_0\approx 17$--$21$ -- entirely from the external readout wire's
self-inductance ($99.3\%$ of $L_p$ from XC3).  If a future,
higher-accuracy kernel resolved $f_1$ above noise, the decision would
shift to row~2 (``inductance floor'') because $\ell_0$ is many orders
of magnitude away from being consistent with zero.  Either way, the
spec's row~1 (favorable thin-annulus scaling) is not the outcome for
this $G$.

\paragraph{What ``this $G$'' means.}

The spec instructs that the readout-wire path runs out to $s=10R$,
down to $z=-2R$, and returns along the $z$-axis.  This wire path
length is $\mathcal O(20 R)$ and dominates $L_p$ at every $\beta$
in the swept range.  The spec acknowledges (Sec.~``External return
path (precise)'') that the wire is ``part of $G$ and the answer
depends on it.''  A wire path whose self-inductance scales as
$\mu_0 R_t\beta$ rather than $\mu_0 R_t$ (e.g.\ a coaxial return that
hugs the inner sheath cylinder) would not produce this $\ell_0$
floor, and a re-run with that $G$ could plausibly fall in row~1.
\emph{This is the natural next step if the toroid topology is of
ongoing interest.}

\paragraph{Inconclusive-flags raised (spec Sec.~``What counts as
inconclusive'').}  Three flags are triggered in our data:
\begin{itemize}
\item $F(\beta)$ is non-monotone and has sign changes at every
$\alpha$, violating flag 1.
\item $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ for the unconstrained $F$ fit is not
statistically meaningful with $f_1$ at noise; the polynomial ansatz
cannot be verified or refuted from these data, partial flag 2.
\item No condition-number violations ($\max\mathrm{cond}=1.8\times 10^7
<10^8$), so flag 3 does not fire.
\end{itemize}
The raw points and fits are preserved in
\texttt{outputs/production\_sweep.csv} and
\texttt{outputs/fit\_results.json}.  We do not paper over the noise:
the report (\texttt{outputs/report.md}) gives the formal
row-3 verdict and the conditional row-2 verdict side by side.

\section{Wire-removal patch (study ii)}
\label{sec:patch}

The patch (\texttt{wire\_removal\_patch.md}, committed as the first
edit of study ii) zeros out the external-readout-wire contribution to
the linear-system RHS, to $L_p$, and to $V_{\rm eff}$.  Mesh
generation, basis, kernel assembly, slit topology, and the
$\Delta\psi\!=\!1$ Dirichlet constraint are unchanged.  The slit BC
remains the only source driving non-trivial sheath current.

\subsection{The $V_{\rm eff}=0$ symmetry result}

\paragraph{Claim.}  For the sheath-only setup, $V_{\rm eff}\equiv 0$
identically, by $\phi$-reflection symmetry of the
antisymmetric-Dirichlet slit BC.

\paragraph{Proof.}  The sheath geometry is invariant under
$\phi\to-\phi$ (the four-piece meridional rectangle reflects to
itself; the slit at $|\phi|<w/2$ maps to itself).  Under this
reflection the slit BC maps as
$\psi(+w/2)=+1/2\to\psi(-w/2)=+1/2$ and
$\psi(-w/2)=-1/2\to\psi(+w/2)=-1/2$, i.e.\ the reflected BC are
$-1\times$ the original.  Since the bilinear energy form is
$\phi$-reflection invariant, the unique energy-minimising
$\psi$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\psi(\phi)=-\psi(-\phi).
\end{equation}
With $\vec K=\nabla_S\psi\times\hat n$ and the surface basis
$(\hat\chi,\hat t)$ behaving as $(\hat\phi,\hat t)$ under reflection
(i.e.\ $\hat\phi\to-\hat\phi$, $\hat t\to\hat t$, $\hat n\to\hat n$
component-wise on the symmetric pieces of the sheath), one finds
\begin{equation}
K_\phi(\phi)=-K_\phi(-\phi),\qquad
K_z(\phi)=+K_z(-\phi),\qquad
K_s(\phi)=+K_s(-\phi).
\end{equation}
In Cartesian, $K_x$ is even and $K_y$ is odd in source-azimuth
$\phi'$.  The kernel $1/|\vec r-\vec r'|$ depends on
$(\phi-\phi')$ only, so under simultaneous reflection of field and
source azimuths
\begin{equation}
A_x(s,-\phi,z)=+A_x(s,\phi,z),\quad
A_y(s,-\phi,z)=-A_y(s,\phi,z).
\end{equation}
Projecting onto $\hat\phi$ at azimuth $\phi$:
\begin{equation}
A_\phi(s,\phi,z)=-\sin\phi\,A_x+\cos\phi\,A_y
\;\Longrightarrow\;
A_\phi(s,-\phi,z)=-A_\phi(s,\phi,z).
\end{equation}
The domain $V_{\rm mag}=\{1<s<1+\beta,\;0<\phi<2\pi,\;0<z<\alpha\}$ is
$\phi$-reflection invariant, so the integral of an odd function
vanishes:
\begin{equation}
\Veff=\frac{R}{\mu_0}\int_{V_{\rm mag}} A_\phi\,s\,ds\,d\phi\,dz=0.
\end{equation}
The numerical result confirms this to machine precision (sweep
values $|F|\sim 10^{-15}$ to $10^{-19}$).

\paragraph{Physical consequence.}  In the spec's stream-function BEM
formulation the slit-driven sheath has no signal-coupled mode in
vacuum.  Any non-zero $\Veff$ in a realistic readout must come from
an \emph{external current path that breaks the $\phi$-reflection
symmetry}.  The v2 spec's wire path does break this symmetry (asymmetric
under $\phi\to-\phi$, since the wire makes a single excursion to
$\phi=+w/2$ and a return at $\phi=-w/2$), which is why study~(i)
produced a non-zero (though tiny) $F$.  In any future design, the
combination of (a) symmetry-breaking and (b) low wire self-inductance
is what determines whether the favorable scaling materialises.

\section{Harmonic-mode fix (study iii)}
\label{sec:harmonic}

The wire-removal patch (study ii) returned $V_{\rm eff}\equiv 0$ by
$\phi$-reflection symmetry of the slit-driven antisymmetric BC.  The
user flagged this as wrong (oracle: $V_{\rm eff}\ne 0$).  Re-examining
the topology of the BEM formulation, the slit-cut sheath is a
topological cylinder, and its first cohomology contains one
non-trivial generator -- a harmonic 1-form representing uniform flow
along the cylinder's long axis (toroidal direction).  My
single-valued-$\psi$ formulation of study (ii) cannot represent this
harmonic mode: $\nabla_S\psi\times\hat n$ is exact, while the
harmonic 1-form is closed but not exact.  Hence study (ii) found
only the linked/poloidal mode, which has $V_{\rm eff}=0$.

\subsection{The harmonic mode}

On the toroid sheath the unit-toroidal-current harmonic 1-form is
\begin{equation}
\vec K_{\rm har}(s,\phi,z) = \frac{c}{s}\hat\phi,
\qquad
c = \frac{1+\beta}{\alpha(2+\beta) + 2(1+\beta)\ln(1+\beta)},
\end{equation}
on every segment of the sheath.  Properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item Closed: $\nabla_S\times\vec K_{\rm har}=0$ on each segment
(check $\partial_s(s K_\phi)=\partial_s c = 0$).
\item Divergence-free: $\nabla_S\cdot\vec K_{\rm har}=0$ on each
segment ($K_\phi$ independent of $\phi$).
\item $K_\phi$ continuous across corners (same value at any
corner, $K_\phi=c/s$).
\item Normalized: $\oint K_\phi\,d\ell = 1$ around the meridional
rectangle.
\end{itemize}
This is the unique axisymmetric closed-not-exact harmonic 1-form on
the cut torus, normalized to unit toroidal current.

\subsection{Variational formulation}

We write $\vec K_{\rm total}=\vec K_{\rm har}+\nabla_S\psi\times\hat n$
and minimise the magnetic energy
\begin{equation}
W=\tfrac{1}{8\pi}\iint\frac{\vec K(\vec r)\cdot\vec K(\vec r')}
{|\vec r-\vec r'|}\,dS\,dS'
\end{equation}
over single-valued $\psi$, with homogeneous Dirichlet $\psi=0$ on
both slit edges (the toroidal current is carried by
$\vec K_{\rm har}$; $\psi$ provides only the Meissner correction).
The variational equation is identical in form to the wire-coupled
study (i), with $\vec A_{\rm har}(\vec c_e) = (1/4\pi)\sum_{e'}
\vec K_{\rm har}^{(e')} A_{e'}/|\vec c_e-\vec c_{e'}|$ replacing
$\vec A_{\rm wire}$ at sheath centroids.  Self-elements use
\begin{equation}
\int_E\frac{dS'}{|\vec c_e-\vec r'|} \approx 2 a \ln\frac{r+b}{a}
+ 2 b \ln\frac{r+a}{b},
\qquad r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2},
\end{equation}
with $a=s\,d\chi$, $b=dt$.

The total inductance and effective volume are
\begin{equation}
L_p = \psi^T A\psi + 2\psi^T c + L_{\rm har,self},
\qquad
V_{\rm eff} = R\!\int_{V_{\rm mag}}A_\phi^{\rm total}\,ds\,d\phi\,dz,
\end{equation}
with $L_{\rm har,self}=(1/4\pi)\sum_j \vec K_{\rm har,j}^T G\,\vec K_{\rm har,j}$.

\subsection{Cross-checks}

\item Convergence: $V_{\rm eff}=0.911$, $L_p=1.28$, stable across

\item XC1: not applicable.
\item XC2 (zero slit): $V_{\rm eff}$ stays at $\approx 0.91$ across

limitation of the $\vec K_{\rm har}$ formulation: $\vec K_{\rm har}$

toroidal current vanishes when the slit closes.  In the wire-driven
spec setup the wire physically cannot push current through a closed

\subsection{What happens at fixed magnet cost (small-$\beta$ limit)}
\label{sec:fixed-cost}

A common point of confusion: the dimensionless $F(\beta)\propto\beta$
does \emph{not} imply $V_{\rm eff}\to 0$ at small $\beta$ when the
comparison is done at fixed magnet cost.  With
$C=2\pi B_{\max}^2 R^3\alpha\ln(1+\beta)\approx 2\pi B^2 R^3\alpha\beta$
for small $\beta$, fixing $C$ gives $R\propto\beta^{-1/3}\to\infty$,
and
\begin{align}
V_{\rm eff} &= R^3\, F(\beta)
            \approx \frac{C}{2\pi\alpha B_{\max}^2\beta}\,f_1\beta
            = \frac{C f_1}{2\pi\alpha B_{\max}^2}
            \quad\text{(constant in $\beta$)},
            \\
L_p &= \mu_0 R\,\ell(\beta)
     \approx \mu_0\left(\frac{C}{2\pi\alpha B_{\max}^2\beta}\right)^{1/3}\ell_0
     \quad\text{(grows as $\beta^{-1/3}$ because $\ell_0\ne 0$)},
            \\
Q^{\rm tor} &\propto \frac{V_{\rm eff}^4}{L_p^2}
            \propto \beta^{2/3} \to 0 \quad(\beta\to 0).
\end{align}
So in the inductance-floor regime $V_{\rm eff}$ stays finite as the
annulus thins (at fixed cost), and the problem is that $L_p$ grows
because $R$ grows while $\ell_0$ doesn't shrink.  In the favorable
regime ($\ell=\ell_1\beta$, no floor) one finds $Q^{\rm tor}\propto
\beta^{-4/3}\to\infty$: that is the regime where the toroid
asymptotically beats the Core.  Our data sits in the inductance-floor
regime, so $Q^{\rm tor}$ at fixed cost \emph{decreases} as $\beta$
decreases.  The toroid is most competitive at the largest $\beta$ in
our sweep ($\beta=0.20$); the crossover table reports $Q$ there.

\paragraph{Caveat on $\ell_0$.}  The reported $\ell_0$ is the
variational minimum of $L_p$ subject to the BC ``$\psi=0$ on both
slit edges,'' which corresponds physically to ``$\vec K_{\rm har}$
is the toroidal current source, $\psi$ corrects only the Meissner
condition, no current leaks across the slit beyond what
$\vec K_{\rm har}$ supplies.''  This is the right BC for a setup
where the current is supplied uniformly along the slit edges; for
localized terminals (as the spec describes), the constraint is
different and $\ell_0$ would differ.  An analytic check against the
closed-torus axisymmetric self-inductance (e.g., the rectangular-
cross-section toroidal-solenoid formula
$L\sim(\mu_0/2\pi)\,\alpha\ln(1+\beta)$ at unit total current) is a
natural sanity test and is left for follow-up.

\subsection*{Adversarial review of the $V_{\rm eff}=0$ symmetry proof}

A third reviewer was asked to find the most damaging objection to the
$\phi$-reflection symmetry argument.  The candidate fatal objection
was: \emph{``$\vec B_0=B_{\max}(R/s)\hat\phi$ is itself a vector
field, and under $\phi\to-\phi$ one has $\hat\phi\to-\hat\phi$, so
$\vec B_0\cdot\vec A_1$ should transform with both factors flipping
sign, making the product even, not odd, and the integral non-zero.''}

\emph{Resolution.}  The reviewer correctly concluded the objection is
not fatal.  The transformation conflates two different concepts: how a
vector field transforms as a geometric object under an active
diffeomorphism, vs.\ how a coordinate component varies as a scalar
function of $\phi$.  The integral
$\Veff=(1/\mu_0)\int \vec B_0\cdot\vec A_1\,dV=
\int(R/s) A_\phi(s,\phi,z)\,s\,ds\,d\phi\,dz=\int A_\phi(s,\phi,z)\,
ds\,d\phi\,dz$ uses the cylindrical $\phi$-component
$A_\phi(s,\phi,z)$ evaluated locally at each point in $V_{\rm mag}$.
The prefactor $R/s$ is $\phi$-independent (manifestly invariant under
reflection).  The proof's claim
